The call

Five parts, said up front.

Every call has the same parts in the same order, so you can hold it to account later.

Buy, sell, wait or avoid, where, when it is wrong, how sure, and the risk.

I

Buy, sell, wait or avoid

One answer, never a hedge. Avoid means the contract failed a check.

II

Entry zone

Where the call makes sense, sized to the token's recent swings.

III

Invalidation

The price at which the call is wrong, set before the fact.

IV

Conviction

One to five. Lower for thin pools, quiet markets, or mixed signals.

V

The main risk

The one thing that would change its mind.

It never invents a number, and it says when a market is too thin to trade.

Is a call financial advice?

No. A call is an automated reading of public market and contract data, made by fixed rules. It says what the numbers show, not what you should do with your money.

How are calls made?

Every four hours Oxva reads price trend and order flow over 5 minutes, 1 hour, 6 hours and 24 hours, runs nine contract checks, and turns that into buy, sell, wait or avoid. The entry zone is sized to recent swings, and the invalidation sits two and a half zones away, at least 3%. After a very large 24-hour move it waits instead of chasing. The rules are published on the Read page.

How is the track record kept honest?

Calls are stored when they are made and graded against the price afterwards. An invalidated call stays invalidated. Calls close after three days with their last grade, and none are deleted: after two weeks they move to a monthly archive and still count in the record.

Who answers in the chat?

A Claude model from Anthropic, given Oxva's live data for the token you ask about. Your messages pass through Oxva's server on Netlify to Anthropic's API to get an answer; Oxva does not store them. It is free with limits: 20 messages an hour per visitor and 40 answers a day for the whole site (resets at 00:00 UTC); the chat page shows what is left today.

Can the wall hold my agent's funds?

Yes. Create an Oxva Vault on Robinhood Chain, fund it, and set the rules. Try it first on testnet with free test ETH. On mainnet it holds real ETH as a beta: the contract is open source, verified on-chain, and passes 21 tests and an automated analysis. A bug could still lose funds. Only put in what you can afford to lose. You can read the code on chain: Oxva's own vault {{LIVE_VAULT_SHORT}} is verified on Blockscout, an exact match with the published source.

Do I need a wallet?

Only for the vault. Calls, markets, chat and reads work without one. For the vault, any browser wallet such as MetaMask or Rabby works, or scan a QR code with your phone wallet; Oxva never sees your keys.
