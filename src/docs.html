What Oxva is

Oxva is one agent with two jobs.

  • It reads the market. Every four hours it makes a call on each token it watches, and every call is graded in public afterwards.
  • It guards every move. The wall checks each transaction a trading agent proposes against rules you set, before anything is signed. On Robinhood Chain those rules run on chain, in an Oxva Vault.

Calls, markets, reads and the chat work without a wallet. You only need a wallet for the vault.

Calls

Every four hours Oxva reads each token it watches and makes one call. A call always has the same five parts, said up front:

Action
BUY, SELL, WAIT or AVOID. One answer, never a hedge. AVOID means the contract failed a check, whatever the market says.
Entry zone
Where the call makes sense, sized to the token's recent swings (between 0.6% and 5% around the price).
Invalidation
The price at which the call is wrong, set before the fact: two and a half zones away from the price, and never closer than 3%.
Conviction
One to five. Lower for thin pools, quiet markets or mixed signals.
Main risk
The one thing that would change its mind.

How a call is made

Oxva reads price trend and order flow over 5 minutes, 1 hour, 6 hours and 24 hours, plus pool depth, and runs the contract checks described under Read a token. Fixed rules turn that into the call. No model sits in that step, and it never invents a number.

Robinhood stock tokens move like stocks, so for them a smaller move counts as strong (5% in 24 hours instead of 20%). Two more rules keep the record honest: after a very large 24-hour move (7.5% for a stock token, 30% for others) a buy or sell becomes a wait instead of chasing it, and while a buy or sell on a token is still being graded the same call is not repeated every four hours.

Tokens watched

On Robinhood Chain, Robinhood’s official stock tokens (NVDA, TSLA, AAPL, META, MSTR, CRCL) and native tokens (PONS, WALLET). Many look-alike stock tokens exist there; only the official “• Robinhood Token” contracts, checked on the explorer, are used. Elsewhere: PEPE, LINK, UNI, AAVE (Ethereum), AERO (Base) and CAKE (BNB Chain).

A call is an automated reading of public data, not financial advice. It says what the numbers show, not what you should do with your money.

Track record

Every call is stored when it is made and graded against the price afterwards. Nothing is deleted: after two weeks a call leaves the live list for a monthly archive, and the record keeps counting it. Each month opens on Calls as cards (/calls/?archive=YYYY-MM), or as JSON at /api/calls?archive=YYYY-MM.

To hear about new batches, follow Oxva on Telegram or X, or add the RSS feed to a reader. The status page shows when the last batch ran.

  • Open: a BUY or SELL younger than 30 minutes, not graded yet.
  • Working: the price has moved the way the call said.
  • Against: the price has moved the other way, but not past the invalidation.
  • Invalidated: the price crossed the invalidation. This is final; it stays invalidated.
  • Watching: WAIT and AVOID calls. They are shown but not counted in the score.

A call is graded for three days, then closes with its last status. The hit rate is the share of graded BUY and SELL calls that are working. See it live on Calls.

The rules changed twice, so the record is also shown per rule version: v1, the first rules; v2 from 23 Nov, stock tokens judged by stock-sized moves; v3 from 26 Nov, no chasing, a 3% minimum stop and one live call per token. Next to the hit rate the site also shows how many calls are still running (their grade can still change), how fast stops were hit, how long calls lasted, and since when the record runs. The overall hit rate is never hidden: the context sits beside it.

Markets

Markets shows every token Oxva watches in one table: the live price and 24-hour change, liquidity and 24-hour volume from the deepest pool, the share of the last day’s trades that were buys, and Oxva’s latest call on it. Prices come from DexScreener; press Refresh for a new read. Read → on a row opens the full read of that token.

Read a token

On Read a token, paste any token address on Robinhood Chain, Ethereum, BNB Chain, Base, Arbitrum or Polygon. You get a market read and the contract checks, in one answer.

The nine contract checks

  • Contract code: is the source code public?
  • Test sell: can the token actually be sold? (simulated where the chain allows it)
  • Buy tax and sell tax: how much a trade loses to fees (two checks).
  • Owner powers: can the owner mint, pause, blacklist or change fees?
  • Liquidity depth: is there enough in the pool to trade?
  • Liquidity locked: can the liquidity be pulled?
  • Holder spread: is supply concentrated in a few wallets?
  • Market activity: is anyone actually trading?

A failing critical check (a token that cannot be sold, a high tax, or dangerous owner powers) makes the answer AVOID, whatever the market says.

Sharing

Every call card, every read and every open vault has a Share button. On a phone it opens your share menu (WhatsApp, Telegram, X…); on a computer it copies the link. The link opens the same token or vault, and shows an Oxva preview card in chat apps. Copying only the contract address shares plain text, with no card.

The wall

A signature proves who sent a transaction; it says nothing about limits, recipients or approvals. The wall checks those first. Every transaction an agent proposes gets one of three answers:

  • Allowed: within the rules. Signed and sent, exactly the payload that was checked.
  • Needs approval: above your threshold. Held until you confirm it.
  • Denied: against the rules. Never signed, never sent, and the reason is logged.

The rules: an hourly budget, a cap per transaction, an approval threshold, an allowlist of where money may go, maximum slippage, and replay protection. Try them in the browser simulator; they run for real in an Oxva Vault.

Oxva Vault

A vault is a smart contract on Robinhood Chain that you own. Your AI agent can spend from it, but only inside your rules, and the rules are enforced on chain, not on Oxva’s server.

Hourly budget
How much ETH the agent may send per hour without asking.
Cap per call
The most a single call may send, even with your approval.
Approve above
Above this amount, a call waits for you to approve or reject it.
Allowlist
The only addresses the agent may send to or call. Everything else is refused.
Tokens
The agent can never move tokens (transfer, approve, permit, NFT transfers); only you can take tokens out.
Pause and withdraw
You can pause the agent and withdraw everything at any time.

Create one

  1. Open Vault and connect a wallet.
  2. Choose Testnet (free test ETH from the faucet) to try it first, or Mainnet beta for real ETH.
  3. Set the agent address, the rules and the allowlist, then create it. Your wallet signs and pays the gas; you become the owner.
  4. The page checks that the code at the address is exactly Oxva’s build and shows Verified Oxva code.

Want to see one first? Open Oxva’s own example vault with its full history, or read its code on Blockscout: {{LIVE_VAULT}}, verified on Blockscout and Sourcify as an exact match with the published source.

Mainnet is a beta. Security: open source, verified on-chain, 21 tests and automated analysis. A bug could still lose funds, there is no balance limit, and nobody can recover funds or undo a transaction. Only put in what you can afford to lose.

Automated analysis

On 27 November 2026 the contract was run through two automated analysers, Slither (Trail of Bits) and Solhint, and every finding was reviewed by hand. Result: no high or medium findings. There were six low ones (a withdrawal or agent address could be set to zero by the owner, and the hourly budget window uses the block time) and four informational ones (one small piece of assembly, three low-level calls that all check their result). None needs a change to the contract. Slither runs again automatically whenever the contract changes.

Wallets

Use Connect wallet at the top of any page.

  • Browser wallets: every wallet installed in your browser is listed (MetaMask, Rabby, Coinbase Wallet, OKX, Trust, Rainbow, Phantom and others).
  • Scan with your phone: shows a WalletConnect QR code; scan it with your wallet app.
  • On a phone: open Oxva inside your wallet app’s browser with the links in the picker.

Oxva never sees your keys and will never ask for your seed phrase or private key. Anyone who does is a scammer.

Chat

On Chat, ask about any token or about Oxva’s calls. A Claude model from Anthropic answers from Oxva’s live data, and says so when the data is not there. Your messages pass through Oxva’s server to Anthropic’s API to get an answer; Oxva does not store them.

To keep it running for everyone there are limits: by default 20 messages an hour per visitor, 40 answers a day for the whole site (it resets at 00:00 UTC), and 1,200 characters a message. The chat page shows how many answers are left today.

$OXVA

There is no $OXVA token. A token bound to this agent is an idea, not a product: nothing has launched, no contract exists, and no sale is planned. If it ever launches, it will be announced on the Oxva site first, with its contract address.

Anyone offering $OXVA today is not Oxva. Do not send money to anyone selling it.

$OXVA is live on Robinhood Chain. The only official contract is {{TOKEN_CA}} . Check it on the explorer, follow the price on the chart, or run the nine contract checks on it with Read a token.

Oxva does not make calls on its own token, so it never appears on the Calls page or in the track record. The chat explains the contract checks but gives no price predictions or buy advice about it.

Robinhood Chain has many look-alike tokens: any other $OXVA contract is not Oxva. This is not financial advice. A new token is very risky and can lose all its value.

For developers

Oxva's calls and track record are public data, free to read from any website, app, bot or script. No key is needed and any origin may call it (CORS is open). Please cache: the data changes every four hours, and the API itself is cached for about two minutes.

Calls and the record

GET https://oxvaagent.com/api/calls
  • record: every call ever made, archived ones included: total, graded, working, against, invalidated, waits, avoids, hitRate (percent of graded buy and sell calls that are working).
  • latest: the newest call on each token Oxva watches. calls: the newest 120 calls.
  • Each call: sym, chain, address, action (BUY, SELL, WAIT or AVOID), conviction (1 to 5), price, entry [low, high], invalidation, reason, risk, status (open, working, against, invalidated, watching), final, at (Unix ms) and now (the latest price, time and move in percent).
  • batches: one row per four-hour slot, with missed slots marked; updatedAt, nextCallAt, universe (the tokens watched), rules (the numbers the rules use) and archive (the months archived).
const d = await fetch("https://oxvaagent.com/api/calls").then((r) => r.json());
console.log(d.record.hitRate, d.latest.map((c) => c.sym + " " + c.action));

Archive

GET https://oxvaagent.com/api/calls?archive=2026-11

Calls older than two weeks, one month at a time ({ month, calls }). The months that exist are listed in archive.

Feed

GET https://oxvaagent.com/calls.xml

An RSS feed of the newest 50 calls, for feed readers and bots: a new item whenever a batch runs.

Fair use

Read as often as you need, but keep it polite: once a minute is plenty. Fields may be added; the ones above stay. Calls are automated readings, not advice: if you show them to others, say so, and link back to Oxva. The terms apply. The site's code is public on GitHub.

Status and terms
